One case, opened to closed

The food and stock are different from client accounts. The sequence of the work is the same, so each status below shows where I have already done it.

1 · Open: verify

Check the documents before anything starts

UK employers must see proof of a person's right to work before they start. I checked and filed ID and visa documents for new hires. Onboarding a client follows the same order of steps, with different documents and rules.

From the other side of the counter, I completed KYC as a Binance client and funded my own account, so I have also been the person waiting for approval.

2 · Watch: monitor

Look before anyone reports a problem

Shake Shack sets a 13-point travel path: a fixed walk from the front door through the dining room, kitchen, chillers and staff area. I walked it every 1.5 to 2 hours, so a shortage or a compliance slip showed up early.

A live queue of accounts needs the same routine, run on a schedule with no reminder from anyone.

3 · Escalate

Know which problems should leave your desk

I investigated till, stock and refund irregularities through to an outcome, and kept formal records under the framework from Croner, the UK employment-law adviser. Anything unusual or risky went to the area manager, HR or head office.

What the journal taught me

Speed after a loss is a warning sign

One stop-out on XRP was followed 36 seconds later by the opposite trade, at the same level and the same size. My journal now tags trades like that, and I review them apart from the rest.

At the desk: I can recognise this pattern in account activity.

A stop-out judges the entry

On one idea I pressed the button three times in a night and was stopped each time. The same idea, entered once the next day with a better fill and a tighter stop, ran to target. The analysis had not changed.

At the desk: I would read a client's whole sequence, then the last order.

A stop belongs beyond the move

On BCH, one stop sat just past a retest band and was tagged to the cent. The next, placed clear of its band, survived a run that came within 0.20 of it. Being right on direction did not save the first.

At the desk: I would check where a client's stop sat before answering.

Check the calendar before the entry

My note after one loss said the fix was a ten-second calendar check. On 12 August, CPI landed exactly on forecast and crypto still fell in the first hour. My long stopped out 17 minutes after the release.

At the desk: I would prepare for margin questions on release days.

Four cases from the floor

Systems

A tracker in place of memory

Twelve tabs, one for complaints and one for delivery at each of six sites, gave every problem a place to live. My own routine at each complaint was to go to the guest personally, put it right, log it, fix the cause and follow up later.

Pressure

The patties that nearly ran out

After a big trading day, a delivery came up short. A forecast at 11:00 showed beef running out by about 18:00 and buns by about 19:00. Friends at other sites sent buns. No one could deliver beef, so I called in cover and drove about 1.5 hours each way. We had 15 patties left when I got back, and traded normally until close.

Youngest at the table

At the Fresh Bake launch, senior colleagues often tried their own approach first. Many of my ideas were adopted after those attempts.
